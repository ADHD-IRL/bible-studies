That is arguably one of the most beautiful and well-known definitions of love ever written. It shifts the focus from love as a fleeting *feeling* to love as a series of *actions and choices*.

By defining what love *is* (patient, kind) alongside what it *is not* (envious, boastful, resentful), these verses set a very high bar for how we treat one another. It's often used in weddings, but its core message is a challenge for any relationship: friendships, family, or even how we treat ourselves.

## A Few Thoughts on the Text

- **The "Record of Wrongs":** This is often the hardest part for people. It suggests that true love involves a level of forgiveness that doesn't "weaponize" the past during future arguments.
- **The Context:** Interestingly, when Paul wrote this to the people of Corinth, he wasn't writing to a happy couple; he was writing to a community that was arguing and divided. He was trying to show them a "more excellent way" to coexist.
