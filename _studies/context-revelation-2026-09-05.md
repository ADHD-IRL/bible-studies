## Overview

The Book of Revelation is widely dated to the late first century AD, likely during the reign of the Roman Emperor Domitian (c. 81-96 AD). This was a period of mounting tension between the burgeoning Christian movement and the Roman imperial cult, which demanded absolute loyalty and divine honors for the Emperor. The author, John, wrote from exile on the island of Patmos to address seven churches in Asia Minor, seeking to provide hope and theological perspective to believers facing state-sponsored persecution and social ostracization.

## Key Themes

- The imperial cult of Rome required citizens to participate in public worship rituals, which presented a core conflict for early Christians who viewed Christ as the only legitimate sovereign.
- The literary style is apocalyptic, employing highly symbolic, vivid imagery and numerology to convey spiritual truths about the nature of evil, divine justice, and the ultimate triumph of God's kingdom.
- Social and economic pressures, such as the requirements of trade guilds that involved pagan sacrifices, marginalized Christians and forced them to choose between their faith and their livelihood.

## Practical Application

1. **c. AD 64, Neronian Persecution:** The Great Fire of Rome leads Emperor Nero to blame and brutally persecute the Christian community, setting a precedent for state-sanctioned hostility.
2. **c. AD 70, Destruction of Jerusalem:** Roman legions under Titus destroy the Second Temple, profoundly reshaping Jewish and Christian identity and theology.
3. **c. AD 81, Ascension of Domitian:** Domitian becomes Emperor and vigorously promotes the imperial cult, likely triggering the climate of persecution reflected in Revelation.
4. **c. AD 95, Date of Revelation:** Scholars widely posit this as the time John composed the apocalypse while exiled on Patmos during the height of Roman pressure.
