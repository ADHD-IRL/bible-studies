## Overview

Proverbs 9:10 says, "The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom." This study looks closely at the Hebrew words behind that sentence to see what the fear of the Lord is, why it comes first, and what it produces. Scripture shows that this fear is not a cringing dread that drives people away from God. It is a reverent awe that draws us near to Him, and it becomes the foundation of courage and peace. The person who fears God rightly no longer needs to be ruled by the fear of people, of headlines or of an unstable world. This study opens a month of studies on the fear of the Lord, courage and the peace God gives, and it builds on the earlier study [Fear of God]({{ '/studies/the-church-has-lost-its-fear-of-god-2026-11-21/' | relative_url }}).

## Theological Analysis

Proverbs 9:10 stands near the close of the long introduction to Proverbs (chapters 1 to 9), and it echoes the book's opening line in 1:7. The repetition is deliberate. The whole introduction is framed by the fear of the Lord, telling the reader that every proverb that follows assumes this starting point. Wisdom in Scripture is not first a matter of intelligence or experience. It begins with a right relationship to God.

The second half of the verse explains the first. "The knowledge of the Holy One is understanding." To fear the Lord is to know Him as He truly is: holy, set apart, morally perfect and infinitely great. When we see God clearly, everything else falls into its proper size. That is why this fear is the beginning of wisdom. A person who has not reckoned with God has built life on the wrong foundation, however clever the building.

Exodus 20:20 holds the paradox that unlocks the whole theme. Moses tells Israel at Sinai, "Do not fear," and in the same breath says God came "that His fear may be before you." There is a fear that God removes and a fear that God gives. The terror that makes people flee from Him gives way to a reverent awe that keeps them close and keeps them from sin. The fear of the Lord is the fear of a loved child who would not grieve a holy Father, not the fear of a slave who expects only punishment.

This reverent fear does something remarkable to every other fear. Isaiah 8:12-13 tells God's people not to be afraid of what the surrounding nation fears, and instead to let the Lord of hosts be their fear and their dread. One great fear crowds out many small ones. Proverbs 14:26 draws the conclusion: "In the fear of the LORD there is strong confidence." The early church shows it in practice. Acts 9:31 describes believers "walking in the fear of the Lord and in the comfort of the Holy Spirit," and in that same verse they "had peace." Fear of God and peace from God are not opposites. They travel together.

This is the key to boldness. People fall silent about Jesus because they fear the reactions of others more than they reverence God. When the Holy One becomes larger in our eyes, the opinions of people grow smaller, and courage to speak becomes possible.

## Historical & Cultural Context

Proverbs belongs to the wisdom literature of Israel, much of it gathered under Solomon (Proverbs 1:1) and later collected by others, including the men of Hezekiah (Proverbs 25:1). Other nations of the ancient Near East, such as Egypt and Mesopotamia, also produced wisdom writings full of practical advice about work, speech and family life. What sets Israel's wisdom apart is its foundation. Proverbs does not root wisdom in human tradition or in the king's authority but in the fear of the LORD, using God's covenant name. Wisdom is tied to the God who rescued Israel and gave His law.

Chapter 9 pictures two women calling to passersby. Wisdom has built her house and set her table (9:1-6), and Folly sits at her door calling the simple to stolen pleasures that lead to death (9:13-18). Verse 10 sits between these two invitations and explains how to answer Wisdom's call. The way into her house is the fear of the Lord.

## Practical Application

1. Begin each day by reminding yourself who God is. Read a psalm that describes His holiness and greatness before you check the news.
2. Name the fears that most often silence you, such as rejection, ridicule or losing standing at work, and set each one beside the greatness of God.
3. When you feel pressure to stay quiet about Jesus, ask whose approval matters most to you in that moment.
4. Let reverence shape your choices. Before a decision, ask what would honor the Holy One rather than what would impress people.
5. Pray for the kind of fear that leads to peace, as the early church walked "in the fear of the Lord and in the comfort of the Holy Spirit."
6. Memorize Proverbs 9:10 and Proverbs 14:26 this week and repeat them when anxiety rises.

## Greek & Hebrew Word Study

1. **"yirah" [Hebrew H3374]: "fear"**
   Fear, reverence or awe. When joined to the LORD, it describes the right response of a creature to a holy Creator: humble respect, wonder and a desire not to offend Him. It includes trembling, but it is the trembling of worship rather than flight.

2. **"techillah" [Hebrew H8462]: "beginning"**
   A starting point or first step. Proverbs 9:10 uses this word, while Proverbs 1:7 uses "reshit" [H7225], which can also mean the first or chief part. Together they teach that the fear of the Lord is both where wisdom starts and its most important part.

3. **"chokmah" [Hebrew H2451]: "wisdom"**
   Skill for living. The word is used elsewhere for the skill of craftsmen (Exodus 31:3). Biblical wisdom is the skill of living life well before God.

4. **"qedoshim" [Hebrew H6918]: "the Holy One"**
   The word is plural in Hebrew, often understood as a plural of majesty pointing to the fullness of God's holiness. To know the Holy One is the heart of understanding.

5. **"binah" [Hebrew H998]: "understanding"**
   Discernment, the ability to see the difference between things. Knowing God gives the clarity to tell what truly matters from what only seems to matter.

6. **"phobos" [Greek G5401]: "fear"**
   The New Testament word used in Acts 9:31 for walking "in the fear of the Lord." It carries the same idea of reverent awe, now in a church filled with the Holy Spirit and at peace.

## Apologetics & Deeper Study

Some read the call to fear God as proof that the God of the Bible rules by terror. Proverbs 9:10 points the other way. The fear it describes leads to wisdom, understanding, confidence, refuge and life (Proverbs 14:26-27). A God who ruled only by terror would drive His people away. The God of Scripture invites them into His house and sets a table for them (Proverbs 9:1-6). Reverent fear is the fitting response to a being who is both infinitely great and perfectly good.

The theme also shows the unity of the Bible. The fear of the Lord runs from Sinai (Exodus 20:20) through the wisdom books (Job 28:28, Psalm 111:10, Proverbs 9:10) and the prophets (Isaiah 8:13) into the life of the early church (Acts 9:31). Peter quotes Isaiah 8 when he tells believers not to fear threats but to set apart Christ as Lord in their hearts (1 Peter 3:14-15). The same reverence that grounded Israel grounds the church, now centered on Jesus.

### Common Objections & Responses

1. **Objection:** "Perfect love casts out fear, so Christians should not fear God at all."

   **Response:** 1 John 4:18 speaks of the fear that "involves torment," the dread of punishment. Scripture still calls believers to reverent fear (Acts 9:31, 2 Corinthians 7:1, 1 Peter 1:17). Love removes terror and deepens awe. The two belong together.

2. **Objection:** "Fearing God will make people anxious and timid."

   **Response:** Scripture teaches the opposite. "In the fear of the LORD there is strong confidence" (Proverbs 14:26). When God is our fear, lesser fears lose their power (Isaiah 8:12-13). The fear of the Lord produces boldness, not timidity.

3. **Objection:** "Wisdom comes from education and experience, not religion."

   **Response:** Proverbs values learning and experience, but it says they rest on a deeper foundation. Knowledge without reverence for God can be used for harm. The fear of the Lord gives wisdom its direction and keeps it from becoming folly.

### Supporting Evidence

1. **A Repeated Biblical Principle**
   The statement that the fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom or knowledge appears in Proverbs 1:7, Proverbs 9:10, Psalm 111:10 and in similar form in Job 28:28, marking it as a central truth of Israel's wisdom.

2. **The Structure of Proverbs**
   The fear of the Lord frames the introduction of Proverbs, appearing at its start (1:7) and near its close (9:10), and returns at the end of the book in the praise of the woman who fears the LORD (31:30).

3. **The Witness of the Early Church**
   Acts 9:31 records a church that walked in the fear of the Lord, enjoyed peace and grew in number, showing that reverent fear and spiritual strength go together.
