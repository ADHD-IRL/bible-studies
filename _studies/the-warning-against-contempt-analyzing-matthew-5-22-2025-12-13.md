**Passage Focus:** Matthew 5:22 (Part of the Sermon on the Mount)

## 1. Context of the Passage: Heart vs. Action

Jesus uses this teaching to deepen the understanding of God's moral law, showing that sin begins in the **condition of the heart**, not just in outward actions.

- **The Old Teaching:** "You shall not murder." (The Pharisees focused on avoiding the physical act.)
- **The Deeper Moral Reality:** Jesus reveals that **harboring anger or contempt** in the heart is just as spiritually dangerous as the act of murder, as both spring from the same root of hatred.
- **The Verse's Hierarchy of Offense:** "Whoever is angry with his brother without cause shall be in danger of the judgment… whoever says, 'You fool!' shall be in danger of hell fire."

## 2. Meaning of the Insult "You Fool"

The condemnation is focused on the **spirit and judgment** behind the insult, not just the word itself.

- **The Greek Word:** The original word translated as "fool" is ***moros***, which means "moron" or "worthless."
- **Cultural Significance:** In that culture, calling someone *moros* was a profound statement of **moral worthlessness**, implying the person was beyond redemption or unworthy of God's grace.
- **Jesus' Intent:** Jesus is addressing the **contemptuous spirit** in the heart. When we view others as hopeless, beneath us, or worthless, we align ourselves with a judging spirit rather than a forgiving one.

## 3. "In Danger of Hell Fire"

- **The Term:** The phrase *"hell fire"* translates the word ***Gehenna***.
- **The Image of Gehenna:** This was a literal valley outside Jerusalem associated with burning refuse and, historically, child sacrifice. Jesus used it as a powerful image of **utter corruption, final judgment, and separation from God**.
- **The Warning:** This warning emphasizes that an **unrepentant heart of hatred and contempt** leads to spiritual ruin, not merely temporary social conflict.

## 4. Spiritual Application for Today

Jesus calls us to a **higher righteousness**: a transformation of our hearts that dictates our behavior.

- **The Heart-Word Connection:** Our words are a direct revelation of the state of our hearts (Luke 6:45).
- **The Call:** Anger and contempt destroy relationships and profoundly offend God's loving character.
- **True Discipleship:** It requires us to **value others as God values them** (created in His image), even when we are wronged or feel justified in our anger.
- **The Response:** This is a call to **examine our hearts**, repent of any bitterness, and allow the Holy Spirit to fill us with Christ's love and humility so that our words bring **life, not destruction.**
