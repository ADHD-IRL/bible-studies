This study focuses on a single, powerful verse that serves as a cornerstone for how believers should interact with one another in the body of Christ.

## Key Verse

> "We who are strong ought to bear with the failings of the weak and not to please ourselves."
>
> Romans 15:1 (NIV)

## I. Context and Background

- **Book:** Romans, written by the Apostle Paul.
- **Theme of Romans 14 & 15:** These chapters deal with controversial issues in the early church, particularly differences in personal convictions (such as dietary laws and observing special days). Paul addresses the relationship between Christians with "strong" consciences (who understand their freedom in Christ) and those with "weak" consciences (who still struggle with scruples or legalistic thinking).
- **Bridge from Chapter 14:** Romans 14 concludes with the principle that everything we do must be an act of faith. Romans 15:1 takes this concept and turns it into a practical, selfless command for those who are mature.

## II. Word-by-Word Exploration

| Greek Word (Transliteration) | English Translation | Meaning & Significance |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| **Hēmeis** | We | Paul includes himself, it's a universal requirement for mature believers. |
| **hoi dynatoi** | the strong | Refers to those whose faith is mature, who are fully convinced of their freedom in Christ (e.g., they know all foods are clean). |
| **opheilomen** | ought / we are under obligation | Not a suggestion, but a **moral duty** or debt. It's what *must* be done. |
| **bastazein** | to bear with / to carry | Means to **lift up and carry** a heavy burden. It suggests patience, endurance, and practical help. |
| **ta asthenēmata** | the failings / infirmities | The weaknesses, lack of strength, or conscientious scruples of others. This is the **burden** the strong are to carry. |
| **tōn adynatōn** | of the weak | Those who are still struggling with doubt, fear, legalism, or a less-developed understanding of Christian freedom. |
| **mē heautois areskein** | and not to please ourselves | The opposite of bearing with the weak. A direct command to **avoid self-gratification** or making our comfort/preference the priority. |

## III. Reflection Questions

### A. Defining "Strong" and "Weak"

1. **Self-Examination:** In what areas of your Christian life do you consider yourself "strong" (confident in your freedom, firm in your knowledge)? In what areas might you be "weak" (struggling with doubt, overly scrupulous, easily tempted)?
2. **Identifying the "Weakness":** What are some modern "failings of the weak" we might be tempted to dismiss (e.g., differences in entertainment choices, political views, worship styles, or sensitivity to certain forms of speech)?
3. **The Danger of "Strength":** If you are "strong," what is the primary temptation you face when dealing with a "weak" brother or sister (based on the phrase, *"...and not to please ourselves"*)?

### B. The Command to "Bear With"

1. **The Nature of the Burden:** "Bear with" (*bastazein*) means to **carry a burden**. What does it look like *practically* to "carry" the failings, fears, or struggles of another believer in your small group or church?
2. **Christ's Example:** How did Jesus "bear with" our weaknesses and failings? (See also Matthew 11:28-30 and Galatians 6:2).
3. **The Alternative:** What happens to the unity of the church when the "strong" refuse to "bear with" the "weak" and instead insist on exercising all their rights and freedoms?

### C. The Call to Selflessness

1. **Motivation:** Paul says we "ought" (*opheilomen*) to do this, suggesting a moral obligation. Where does this obligation come from? (Hint: See Romans 15:2-3).
2. **Inversion of Rights:** The world teaches us to stand up for our rights and personal happiness ("to please ourselves"). How does Paul's command radically contradict this worldly value system?
3. **Application:** Think of a specific situation in your life where you were tempted to put your own preference (your "right" or "freedom") ahead of the spiritual growth or comfort of another believer. How could you have applied Romans 15:1?

## IV. Conclusion and Prayer Points

**Summary Thought:** Christian liberty is a freedom **from** sin, not a freedom **to** cause a brother or sister to stumble. Maturity is demonstrated not by the knowledge we possess, but by the love and self-restraint we exercise for the benefit of others.

**Prayer Focus:**

1. **Ask for Humility:** Pray that God would grant you the humility to recognize your own potential for arrogance in the areas where you are "strong."
2. **Ask for Love:** Pray for an outpouring of the love of Christ, which enables you to see the "weak" as Jesus does, and to genuinely desire their edification over your own comfort.
3. **Ask for Strength to Carry:** Pray for the patience and practical strength required to "carry" the burdens and misunderstandings of others without complaining or becoming resentful.
4. **Pray for Church Unity:** Pray that your church would be characterized by this selfless, "bearing-with" attitude, reflecting Christ's love to the world.
