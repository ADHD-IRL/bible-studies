The genealogical lineage that led to Jesus according to the Biblical accounts.

## The Lineage Leading to Jesus

The Bible presents two genealogies of Jesus, one in the Gospel of Matthew and one in the Gospel of Luke. These genealogies trace different lines but both establish Jesus's descent from key figures in Jewish history.

## The Patriarchal Line

The lineage begins with Abraham, the father of the Jewish nation:

- Abraham fathered Isaac with his wife Sarah in their old age
- Isaac fathered Jacob (also called Israel)
- Jacob had twelve sons who became the heads of the twelve tribes of Israel
- Judah, the fourth son of Jacob, was specifically chosen as the tribal line through which the Messiah would come

## From Judah to David

The line continued through the tribe of Judah:

- Judah fathered Perez (with Tamar)
- The line continued through Perez to Hezron, Ram, Amminadab, Nahshon, Salmon
- Salmon married Rahab and fathered Boaz
- Boaz married Ruth (a Moabite woman) and fathered Obed
- Obed fathered Jesse
- Jesse fathered David, who became the second king of Israel

## The Royal Line from David

God made a covenant with David that his throne would be established forever, marking his dynasty as the one through which the Messiah would come:

- David fathered Solomon with Bathsheba
- Solomon was followed by a succession of kings of Judah including Rehoboam, Abijah, Asa, Jehoshaphat, and others
- The royal line continued through the Kingdom of Judah until the Babylonian exile

## Post-Exile to Joseph and Mary

After the Babylonian exile, the line continued through Zerubbabel, who led the first group of Jews returning from exile:

- The line continued through several generations until Joseph, who became the legal father of Jesus
- Mary, the mother of Jesus, was also descended from David, likely through another son named Nathan (according to Luke's genealogy)

## Matthew's and Luke's Genealogies

Matthew's genealogy (Matthew 1:1-17) traces Jesus's legal lineage through Joseph, emphasizing Jesus as the heir to David's throne. It is organized into three sets of fourteen generations:

- From Abraham to David
- From David to the Babylonian exile
- From the exile to Jesus

Luke's genealogy (Luke 3:23-38) appears to trace Mary's ancestry, going all the way back to Adam and ultimately to God. This emphasizes Jesus's identity as the "son of Adam" and "Son of God."

## Notable Aspects of Jesus's Genealogy

Several aspects of Jesus's genealogy are significant:

1. It includes Gentile women (Tamar, Rahab, Ruth, and indirectly Bathsheba), showing God's plan included all nations
2. It includes people with moral failures (such as Judah, David, and others), demonstrating God's grace
3. The genealogy fulfills prophecies that the Messiah would be:
   - From the seed of Abraham (Genesis 22:18)
   - From the tribe of Judah (Genesis 49:10)
   - From the line of David (2 Samuel 7:12-16)
   - Born in Bethlehem, David's city (Micah 5:2)
4. The virgin birth, with Mary conceiving by the Holy Spirit, fulfilled Isaiah's prophecy (Isaiah 7:14) while maintaining the legal lineage through Joseph

This genealogical framework establishes Jesus as the legitimate heir to David's throne and the fulfillment of God's promises to Abraham, positioning him as the long-awaited Messiah according to Biblical prophecy.
