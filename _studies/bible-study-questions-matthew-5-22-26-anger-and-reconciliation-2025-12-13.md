Including the original Greek words and their meanings clarifies the intensity of Jesus's warning in Matthew 5:22.

## I. Examining the Heart: The Severity of Anger (v. 22)

1. Jesus puts "anger," "Raca," and "You fool" on a spectrum of sin, leading to judgment. The word for "anger" is **orgizomenos** (from *orgē*), meaning deep-seated, simmering resentment or being provoked. The word for "You fool" is **Mōre** (or *mōros*), which implies a moral and spiritual dullness, essentially saying the person is worthless or reprobate.
   - What is the difference between righteous anger (like Jesus clearing the temple) and the kind of contemptuous anger Jesus warns against here?
2. In your own words, how does contempt for another person (using Mōre to dismiss their value) show a breakdown in honoring God, who created that person in His image?
3. Have you ever allowed **orgizomenos** (simmering resentment) in your mind or used a harsh word to dismiss someone's value or opinion? What does this verse challenge you to confess and change in your inner attitude?

## II. The Test of True Worship: Reconciliation (vv. 23-24)

1. Jesus says to leave your gift at the altar and go be reconciled **first**. Why do you think restored relationships are more valuable to God than the act of worship itself?
2. How might "leaving your gift at the altar" apply in your life today (e.g., attending a prayer meeting, reading your Bible, singing a worship song)? Is there a form of "religious activity" you might be engaging in while ignoring a broken relationship?
3. The text says to act when "your brother or sister has something **against you**." Why is it your responsibility to initiate reconciliation even when you feel you are the offended party, or when you believe the other person is more wrong?

## III. The Urgency of Peace-Making (vv. 25-26)

1. Jesus shifts the analogy from the altar to the courtroom, urging us to "settle the matter quickly." What are the practical, spiritual, and emotional consequences of *delaying* reconciliation?
2. In what ways does this picture of the "judge," the "officer," and "prison" highlight the seriousness and increasing cost of unresolved conflict?
3. To "settle the matter quickly" requires humility. What specific fears or pride issues usually prevent you from reaching out to resolve a conflict immediately?

## IV. Living It Out: Application

1. Think about your closest relationships (family, friends, church). Is there anyone who might "have something against you" right now? What **specific, Spirit-led step** will you take this week to pursue peace with them?
2. How can you transform the commitment to relational integrity into an **act of worship** (as opposed to just a chore)?
3. Based on this passage, how should believers treat relational conflicts differently than the world treats them? (e.g., in communication, forgiveness, and urgency)
