The story of the Jewish Temple in the Old Testament is a dramatic arc of divine glory, catastrophic destruction, and a painstaking, decades-long restoration. The history revolves around two central structures: the original architectural marvel built by King Solomon, and the "Second Temple" rebuilt by the returning exiles led by Zerubbabel.

Here is the historical sequence of the Temple's construction, destruction, and rebuilding, as recorded in the Old Testament:

- **The Preparation**
  *~1000 BC*
  King David captures Jerusalem and purchases a threshing floor on Mount Moriah to build an altar. Though David deeply desires to build a permanent house for the Ark of the Covenant, God tells him that his son will be the one to build it. David spends his final years stockpiling vast amounts of gold, silver, bronze, iron, and cedar logs.
- **The First Temple Built**
  *~966-959 BC*
  In the fourth year of his reign, King Solomon begins construction on the Temple. Utilizing David's stockpiled materials and thousands of laborers, the glorious First Temple is completed in just over seven years. At its dedication, the biblical account records that the glory of the Lord physically fills the sanctuary.
- **The Destruction**
  *586 BC*
  After centuries of spiritual decline and idolatry among the kings of Judah, Jerusalem falls to the Babylonian king Nebuchadnezzar. The city walls are breached, the population is exiled, and Solomon's magnificent Temple is looted and burned to the ground, leaving the Jewish people without a central place of worship.
- **The Decree of Cyrus**
  *538 BC*
  Persia conquers Babylon, and King Cyrus the Great issues a historic decree. Fulfilling prophecies made by Jeremiah, Cyrus permits the Jewish exiles to return to Jerusalem specifically to rebuild the "house of God".
- **The Foundation Laid (Zerubbabel's Temple)**
  *536 BC*
  Led by the governor Zerubbabel and the high priest Jeshua, the first wave of returning exiles rebuilds the bronze altar so sacrifices can resume. They soon lay the foundation for the Second Temple. The event brings mixed emotions: the younger generation shouts for joy, while the older men who remembered the grandeur of Solomon's Temple weep loudly.
- **Construction Halted**
  *530-520 BC*
  Local adversaries fiercely oppose the rebuilding effort. They harass the workers and send false accusations of rebellion to the Persian kings. This political pressure successfully forces a royal decree that halts temple construction for roughly a decade.
- **Prophets Rekindle the Work**
  *520 BC*
  The prophets Haggai and Zechariah step forward with strong messages for the complacent Israelites, challenging them for living in comfortable paneled houses while God's house remains a ruin. Empowered by the prophets and authorized by a new decree from King Darius, Zerubbabel resumes the work.
- **The Second Temple Dedicated**
  *516 BC*
  Exactly 70 years after the destruction of the First Temple, the rebuilt Temple is finally completed in the sixth year of King Darius's reign. The exiles celebrate its dedication with immense joy and observe the Passover.

**Note:** Zerubbabel's Temple stood for five centuries. Around 19 BC, King Herod the Great began a massive project to expand and beautify it into the sprawling complex seen in the scale model above. This is the temple that existed during the time of Jesus before being permanently destroyed by the Romans in 70 AD.
