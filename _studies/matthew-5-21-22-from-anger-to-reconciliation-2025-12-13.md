This verse is a strong warning from Jesus about the seriousness of contempt in the heart and on the tongue. It fits into a bigger section where He shows that true righteousness goes much deeper than just avoiding outward sins like murder.

## Title and Text

**Passage:** Matthew 5:21-22

**Theme:** From anger to reconciliation: why contempt is so serious in God's eyes

## 1. Setting in the Sermon on the Mount

- Jesus is explaining what "real righteousness" looks like for those who belong to His kingdom, not just external rule-keeping.
- He moves from the command, "You shall not murder," to the heart attitudes that lead to murder: anger, contempt, and degrading speech.

## 2. From Murder to the Heart

- The religious leaders focused on the outward act: as long as you did not physically kill, you were "safe."
- Jesus shows that sinful anger and hostility toward a brother or sister already put a person under God's searching judgment, because God looks at the heart, not just the hands.

## 3. The Progression of Sinful Speech

- Jesus mentions different expressions: anger, insulting terms like "Raca" (empty-head), and "You fool," showing an increasing level of contempt.
- Saying "You fool" in this context is not casual teasing; it is condemning someone as worthless, beyond hope, and beneath you, which reflects a heart that has set itself up as judge.

## 4. "In Danger of Hell Fire"

- "Hell fire" points to Gehenna, an image of final judgment and separation from God for those who persist in unrepentant sin.
- Jesus is not saying one angry word automatically sends someone to hell, but that a settled, contemptuous heart reveals a spiritual direction that, if unchecked and unrepented, leads away from God and toward judgment.

## 5. Christ's Call to Reconciliation

- In the verses that follow, Jesus calls His disciples to act quickly to make things right with others instead of nursing anger.
- Worship that pleases God includes a heart that is willing to humble itself, seek forgiveness, and restore broken relationships, rather than cling to bitterness.

## 6. Application for Believers Today

- Examine the heart honestly: Is there hatred, resentment, or contempt lurking beneath "small" words?
- Invite the Holy Spirit to reshape attitudes so that words speak life, not destruction, and practice concrete steps of reconciliation (apologizing, forgiving, and seeking peace) even when pride resists.
