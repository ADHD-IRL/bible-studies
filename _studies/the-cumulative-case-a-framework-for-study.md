Here is the comprehensive study table, integrating the specific questions Christians ask with the broader, independent lines of reasoning that form the cumulative case for the faith. This expanded format is designed to serve as a complete reference guide for further study, showing how the philosophical, historical, and personal elements intersect.

| Line of Reasoning | Primary Question Addressed | Core Argument & Evidence | Key References & Study Concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Cosmological & Teleological (The Universe) | What is the evidence for God? | **Cosmological:** The universe has a beginning and therefore requires a spaceless, timeless, immaterial cause. **Teleological:** The physical constants of the universe are precisely fine-tuned for life, pointing to an intelligent designer rather than random chance. | **Concepts:** Kalam Cosmological Argument; Anthropic Principle. **Scholars/Authors:** Thomas Aquinas (*Summa Theologica*); William Lane Craig; Robin Collins (Fine-Tuning). |
| 2. Axiological (Morality & Meaning) | Why is there objective right and wrong? | **Moral Argument:** If objective moral values and duties exist (e.g., injustice is universally wrong), there must be a transcendent standard. God provides the ontological grounding for an objective moral law, saving humanity from sheer moral relativism. | **Concepts:** Objective vs. Subjective Morality; Evolutionary Ethics vs. Transcendent Law. **Scholars/Authors:** C.S. Lewis (*Mere Christianity*); Immanuel Kant (Moral argument formulations); Paul Copan. |
| 3. Historical & Archaeological (The Record) | Are the Gospels historically reliable? | **Textual:** Unprecedented volume of early manuscript copies (bibliographical test) ensures transmission accuracy. **Corroboration:** Secular 1st-century historians and ongoing archaeological discoveries continually verify the locations, cultures, and figures mentioned in the texts. | **Concepts:** Bibliographical Test; Criterion of Embarrassment; Dead Sea Scrolls. **Scholars/Authors:** F.F. Bruce (*The New Testament Documents*); Tacitus, Josephus, Pliny the Younger (Secular sources). |
| 4. Christological (The Person of Jesus) | Is Jesus trustworthy, and did he rise? | **The Trilemma:** Jesus' explicit claims to divinity mean he is either a liar, a lunatic, or Lord; he cannot merely be a "good teacher." **The Resurrection:** The "Minimal Facts" (death by crucifixion, empty tomb, eyewitness accounts, skeptical conversions) are best explained by a physical resurrection, as naturalistic theories fail to account for all facts simultaneously. | **Concepts:** Lewis's Trilemma; The Minimal Facts Approach; 1 Corinthians 15 Creed. **Scholars/Authors:** Gary Habermas & Michael Licona (*The Case for the Resurrection of Jesus*); N.T. Wright. |
| 5. Existential & Experiential (The Personal) | Why is faith important to the human experience? | **Psychological Fit:** The Christian worldview accurately diagnoses the human condition: capable of immense good (*Imago Dei*) but inherently broken and drawn to selfishness (The Fall). **Transformation:** The historical and contemporary testimony of billions who experience radical life changes, peace, and purpose through a relationship with Christ. | **Concepts:** *Imago Dei*; *Fides quaerens intellectum* (Faith seeking understanding); Religious Epistemology. **Scholars/Authors:** St. Augustine (*Confessions*); Blaise Pascal (*Pensées*); Alvin Plantinga. |
