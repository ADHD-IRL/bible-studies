This Bible study aims to provide an easy-to-understand overview of the biblical roots of the conflict involving Israel, focusing on key lineages and prophecies.

## 1. The Descendants: Isaac and Ishmael

The Bible traces two significant bloodlines from Abraham: Isaac and Ishmael.

- **Ishmael's Lineage:** Ishmael was Abraham's first son, born to Hagar, Sarah's maidservant.
  - **Genesis 16:15-16:** "And Hagar bore Abram a son; and Abram called the name of his son, whom Hagar bore, Ishmael. Abram was eighty-six years old when Hagar bore Ishmael to Abram."
  - **God's Promise to Ishmael:** Even though Ishmael was not the child of promise, God promised to bless him and make him a great nation.
    - **Genesis 17:20:** "As for Ishmael, I have heard you; behold, I have blessed him, and will make him fruitful, and will multiply him exceedingly. He shall father twelve princes, and I will make him a great nation."
    - **Genesis 21:13:** "And I will make a nation of the son of the bondwoman also, because he is your offspring."
  - **Ishmael's Character:** The Bible describes Ishmael's relationship with others.
    - **Genesis 16:12:** "He shall be a wild donkey of a man; his hand shall be against every man, and every man's hand against him; and he shall dwell over against all his kinsmen." This verse is often interpreted as prophetic of the ongoing conflict involving his descendants.
- **Isaac's Lineage:** Isaac was Abraham's son born to Sarah, the child of God's promise.
  - **Genesis 21:2-3:** "And Sarah conceived and bore Abraham a son in his old age, at the time of which God had spoken to him. Abraham called the name of his son who was born to him, whom Sarah bore him, Isaac."
  - **The Covenant Promise:** God established His covenant with Isaac, through whom the lineage leading to Jesus would come.
    - **Genesis 17:19:** "God said, 'No, but Sarah your wife shall bear you a son, and you shall call his name Isaac. I will establish my covenant with him as an everlasting covenant for his offspring after him.'"
  - **Israel as God's Chosen People:** The descendants of Isaac, through his son Jacob (whose name was changed to Israel), became God's chosen people.
    - **Deuteronomy 7:6:** "For you are a people holy to the Lord your God. The Lord your God has chosen you to be a people for his treasured possession, out of all the peoples who are on the face of the earth."

## 2. Satan's Attacks on the Bloodline Leading to Jesus Christ

Satan, knowing God's plan for salvation through the Messiah, has consistently tried to disrupt the lineage that would lead to Jesus.

- **Early Attempts to Corrupt the Lineage:**
  - **Genesis 3:15:** Immediately after the Fall, God prophesied about the "seed of the woman" who would crush the serpent's head. Satan's efforts began early to corrupt or destroy this promised lineage.
  - **The Flood (Genesis 6):** Humanity became so corrupt that God brought the flood, saving only Noah and his family, thus preserving the pure lineage.
  - **Abram and Sarai in Egypt/Gerar (Genesis 12, 20):** Pharaoh and Abimelech nearly took Sarah, which would have compromised the promised line through whom Isaac would be born. God intervened.
- **Attacks on Israel, God's Chosen People:**
  - **Pharaoh's Decree (Exodus 1:22):** Pharaoh ordered the killing of all Hebrew male babies, a direct attack on the future of the nation from which the Messiah would come.
  - **Various Enemies in the Old Testament:** Throughout Israel's history, nations like the Amalekites (1 Samuel 15), Philistines (1 Samuel 17), Assyrians (2 Kings 18), and Babylonians (2 Kings 25) sought to conquer and destroy Israel. These attacks can be seen as orchestrated, in part, by Satan to prevent the Messiah's arrival.
  - **Haman's Plot (Esther 3):** Haman's attempt to annihilate all Jews in Persia was a direct attack on the entire Jewish people, the very people from whom Jesus would descend. God's miraculous intervention through Esther saved them.
  - **Herod's Slaughter of the Innocents (Matthew 2:16):** King Herod, prompted by the Magi's search for the "King of the Jews," ordered the killing of all male children two years old and under in Bethlehem. This was a direct, albeit failed, attempt by Satan to eliminate the newborn Jesus.

## 3. Attempts to Destroy Israel and God's Chosen Land

The land of Israel is intrinsically linked to God's covenant and His chosen people. Attacks on the land and the people are often seen as attempts to thwart God's ultimate plan.

- **God's Covenant with the Land:** God promised the land of Canaan to Abraham and his descendants as an everlasting possession.
  - **Genesis 15:18:** "On that day the Lord made a covenant with Abram, saying, 'To your offspring I give this land, from the river of Egypt to the great river, the river Euphrates.'"
  - **Genesis 17:8:** "And I will give to you and to your offspring after you the land of your sojournings, all the land of Canaan, for an everlasting possession, and I will be their God."
- **Prophecies of Israel's Return and Restoration:** Despite periods of exile and scattering, the Bible prophesies Israel's return to their land and their ultimate restoration.
  - **Ezekiel 36:24:** "For I will take you from the nations and gather you from all the countries and bring you into your own land."
  - **Amos 9:14-15:** "I will restore the fortunes of my people Israel, and they shall rebuild the ruined cities and inhabit them; they shall plant vineyards and drink their wine, and they shall make gardens and eat their fruit. I will plant them upon their land, and they shall never again be plucked up out of the land that I have given them," says the Lord your God.
- **Spiritual Warfare and End Times:** Many interpretations of biblical prophecy suggest that the ongoing conflict around Israel has a spiritual dimension, with Satan continuing his efforts to oppose God's plans, especially as the return of Christ draws near.
  - **Zechariah 12:3:** "On that day I will make Jerusalem a heavy stone for all the peoples. All who lift it will surely injure themselves. And all the nations of the earth will gather against it." This verse speaks of a future time when nations will come against Jerusalem.
  - **Revelation 12:13, 17:** Describes Satan's fury against those who keep God's commandments and hold to the testimony of Jesus, which can be seen as extending to God's chosen people.

## 4. The Truth of Biblical Prophecy and the Gospel

The fulfillment of biblical prophecies, particularly concerning Israel, provides compelling evidence for the truth and divine inspiration of the Bible.

- **Prophecy of Israel's Rebirth:** The re-establishment of the nation of Israel in 1948, after nearly 2,000 years of dispersion, is considered by many as a miraculous fulfillment of biblical prophecies.
  - **Isaiah 66:8:** "Who has heard such a thing? Who has seen such things? Can a land be born in one day? Can a nation be brought forth in a moment? For as soon as Zion was in labor, she gave birth to her children."
  - **Jeremiah 30:3:** "For behold, days are coming, declares the Lord, when I will restore the fortunes of my people Israel and Judah, says the Lord, and I will bring them back to the land that I gave to their fathers, and they shall take possession of it."

  This historical event demonstrates God's faithfulness to His promises and His sovereign control over nations and history. The continued existence of the Jewish people, despite numerous attempts to destroy them, also stands as a testament to God's preserving hand.
- **The Reliability of God's Word:** If God's prophecies about Israel have been fulfilled with such precision, it strengthens the belief in the reliability of the entire Bible, including its message of salvation.
  - **2 Peter 1:21:** "For no prophecy was ever produced by the will of man, but men spoke from God as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit."
  - **Isaiah 46:10:** "Declaring the end from the beginning and from ancient times things not yet done, saying, 'My counsel shall stand, and I will accomplish all my purpose.'"

## 5. Understanding the Gospel and Salvation Through Grace Alone

Understanding the biblical narrative concerning Israel and the consistent attempts against the lineage of Jesus helps to illuminate the profound need for the Gospel and God's plan of salvation.

- **The Purpose of Jesus' Coming:** The relentless opposition to the Messiah's lineage underscores the immense importance of Jesus' birth, life, death, and resurrection. He came to defeat Satan's power and provide a way for humanity to be reconciled with God.
  - **Hebrews 2:14:** "Since therefore the children share in flesh and blood, he himself likewise partook of the same things, that through death he might destroy the one who has the power of death, that is, the devil."
  - **1 John 3:8:** "Whoever makes a practice of sinning is of the devil, for the devil has been sinning from the beginning. The reason the Son of God appeared was to destroy the works of the devil."
- **Salvation by Grace Through Faith:** The struggles and failures of humanity, including Israel's periods of disobedience, highlight that salvation cannot be earned through human effort or adherence to the law. It is a gift from God, received through faith in Jesus Christ.
  - **Ephesians 2:8-9:** "For by grace you have been saved through faith. And this is not your own doing; it is the gift of God, not a result of works, so that no one may boast."
  - **Romans 3:23-24:** "for all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God, and are justified by his grace as a gift, through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus."
- **God's Unwavering Love and Plan:** Despite the ongoing conflicts and spiritual battles, God's ultimate plan of salvation and His love for humanity remain steadfast. The covenant with Abraham, the preservation of Israel, and the coming of Jesus all demonstrate God's faithfulness to His promises and His desire for all people to come to Him.
  - **John 3:16:** "For God so loved the world, that he gave his only Son, that whoever believes in him should not perish but have eternal life."

In conclusion, the biblical narrative highlights a long-standing conflict rooted in the distinct lineages of Isaac and Ishmael, intertwined with Satan's strategic attempts to hinder God's redemptive plan through Jesus Christ. The land of Israel and its people remain central to God's promises, making them a focal point of spiritual and earthly contention throughout history and into the future. The fulfillment of these prophecies reinforces the Bible's truth, pointing us to the core message of the Gospel: salvation is a free gift from God, received through faith in Jesus Christ, who conquered the power of sin and death.
