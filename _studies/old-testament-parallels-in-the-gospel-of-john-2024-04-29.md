The Gospel of John in the New Testament draws heavily from Old Testament imagery and prophecies, presenting Jesus as the fulfillment of God's promises and foreshadowing in the Hebrew Scriptures. Here are some key parallels between the Book of John and the Old Testament:

1. **John 1:1-5:** The prologue echoes the creation account in Genesis 1, depicting Jesus as the pre-existent Word (Logos) through whom all things were made. This resonates with the wisdom literature in Proverbs 8:22-31, where Wisdom is personified as being with God from the beginning.
2. **John 1:14:** "The Word became flesh and dwelt among us" alludes to the Shekinah glory of God dwelling with the Israelites in the Tabernacle (Exodus 40:34-38) and Temple (1 Kings 8:10-11).
3. **John 1:29:** John the Baptist identifies Jesus as "the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world." This imagery draws from the Passover lamb (Exodus 12) and the suffering servant of Isaiah 53.
4. **John 3:14-15:** Jesus likens his crucifixion to the bronze serpent lifted up by Moses in the wilderness (Numbers 21:4-9), foreshadowing the salvation he would bring.
5. **John 6:31-35:** Jesus declares himself the true bread from heaven, fulfilling the manna in the wilderness (Exodus 16) and the promised provision foretold in Psalm 78:24-25.
6. **John 7:37-39:** Jesus' invitation for the thirsty to come to him recalls the rock that gushed water in the desert (Exodus 17:6) and the prophetic promise of living waters (Jeremiah 2:13, Ezekiel 47).
7. **John 10:11:** Jesus calls himself the good shepherd, echoing the imagery of God as the shepherd of Israel (Psalm 23, Ezekiel 34).
8. **John 19:36:** The detail that none of Jesus' bones were broken fulfills the Passover lamb requirement in Exodus 12:46 and Numbers 9:12.
9. **John 20:22:** Jesus breathes the Holy Spirit on the disciples, reminiscent of God breathing life into Adam (Genesis 2:7) and the vision of the dry bones in Ezekiel 37.

These are just a few examples of how the Gospel of John intentionally links Jesus' life, ministry, and death to the promises, symbols, and foreshadowing found throughout the Old Testament, presenting him as the long-awaited Messiah and fulfillment of God's redemptive plan.
