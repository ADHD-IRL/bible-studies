*Narrator Script with Associated Scriptures*

## Opening

**NARRATOR:** "There's a moment in every person's life when they come face to face with their sin. But here's the question that determines everything: Are you sorry you got caught... or are you sorry you sinned?"

[PAUSE - let it sink in]

"The difference isn't just important, it's eternal."

> "Godly sorrow brings repentance that leads to salvation and leaves no regret, but worldly sorrow brings death."
> 2 Corinthians 7:10

## The Counterfeit

**NARRATOR:** "Worldly sorrow, that's what the Bible calls it. It's the sorrow of getting caught. The regret over consequences. The fear of punishment. The embarrassment of exposure.

This kind of sorrow says: 'I'm sorry I have to face the music. I'm sorry people know. I'm sorry my life is harder now.'

But listen carefully: this sorrow leads to death. It changes nothing in your heart. It's all about YOU: your reputation, your comfort, your plans."

> "The heart is deceitful above all things and beyond cure. Who can understand it?"
> Jeremiah 17:9

## The Authentic

**NARRATOR:** "But there's another kind of sorrow. The Greeks had a beautiful word for it: METANOIA.

Meta - meaning 'change' or 'after.' Noia - meaning 'mind' or 'heart.' Together: a complete transformation of your inner being.

This is godly sorrow. It says: 'I'm not just sorry I got caught, I'm heartbroken that I hurt You, God. I'm not just sorry for the consequences, I'm devastated by my rebellion against Your love.'

David wrote these words after his sin with Bathsheba. He wasn't making excuses. He wasn't minimizing. He was experiencing metanoia: true heart-change that cries out to God for complete transformation."

> "Create in me a pure heart, O God, and renew a steadfast spirit within me."
> Psalm 51:10

## The Requirement

**NARRATOR:** "Friend, this kind of repentance isn't optional, it's required for salvation. Jesus Himself said it:"

> "Repent and believe the good news!"
> Mark 1:15

**NARRATOR:** "The word Jesus used? Metanoeite: the command form of metanoia. He's saying: 'Let your heart be completely changed, then believe!'

You see, salvation isn't just about escaping hell, it's about being transformed. It's not just about forgiveness, it's about becoming new."

> "Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, the new creation has come: The old has gone, the new is here!"
> 2 Corinthians 5:17

## The Invitation

**NARRATOR:** "So let me ask you again: When you think about your sin, what kind of sorrow fills your heart?

If it's worldly sorrow, if you're just sorry about the mess, the consequences, the embarrassment, then cry out to God for true metanoia. Ask Him to break your heart over what breaks His.

But if the Holy Spirit has already been working in you, if you feel genuine grief over your rebellion against a loving God, then know this:"

> "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just and will forgive us our sins and purify us from all unrighteousness."
> 1 John 1:9

**NARRATOR:** "God is ready. Jesus paid the price. The Holy Spirit is calling. This metanoia, this heart-change, is available to you right now."

## Closing

**NARRATOR:** "Don't wait for a more convenient time. Don't wait until you've cleaned up your act. Come to Jesus with genuine repentance, with metanoia, and watch Him make you new.

The heart that truly grieves over sin is the heart that's ready for salvation."

> "The Lord is not slow in keeping his promise, as some understand slowness. Instead he is patient with you, not wanting anyone to perish, but everyone to come to repentance."
> 2 Peter 3:9

- 2 Peter 3:9: God's patience and desire for repentance
