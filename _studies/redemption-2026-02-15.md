Helping the Christian lay reader navigate faith, find true surrender, and live in obedience to experience God's redemption.

## From the Beginning: God's Design for Peace

From the very beginning, God designed humanity to live in perfect peace and harmony with Him. His intent was for us to experience the wholeness, rest, and flourishing that comes from walking in obedience to His loving ways. Sin, however, shattered that original design, introducing brokenness, striving, and separation from God into the human experience.

## The Unavoidable Issue of Sin

While the Bible makes clear that sin separates us from God and robs us of true peace, we often don't grasp sin's magnitude and insidious nature. Sin is rejecting or ignoring God in the world He created, rebelling against Him by living without regard for His commands, and failing to trust Him to make us truly happy.

Even as redeemed believers, we cannot simply wish away the sins that corrupt our human nature. Like Paul, we struggle with the reality that "nothing good dwells in me, that is, in my flesh...For I do not do the good I want, but the evil I do not want is what I keep on doing" (Romans 7:18-19). Ignoring or minimizing our sinful tendencies only gives them more power over us.

Yet God does not leave us powerless against the force of sin. The path to redemption begins with sincere recognition and confession of our brokenness. The Psalmist models this vulnerability: "When I kept silent, my bones wasted away...I acknowledged my sin to you and did not cover up my iniquity...And you forgave the guilt of my sin" (Psalm 32:3-5). In baring our souls before the Lord, we access His mercy and grace to heal and transform us.

## The First Step: Surrendering to the Redeemer

Even after salvation, we are called to continually submit to the Lordship of Christ. Romans 12:1-2 charges us to be "living sacrifices," no longer being "conformed to this world" but being "transformed by the renewal of your mind." Just as Jesus demonstrated full surrender in Gethsemane, "Not my will, but yours be done" (Luke 22:42), we must daily crucify our fleshly desires and yield to the will of God.

This level of surrender can feel radical, challenging our self-sufficiency and pride. We prefer the illusion of control rather than entrusting everything to our Heavenly Father. Like the rich young ruler, the cost of surrender gives us pause (Mark 10:17-22). Yet Jesus clarifies, "Whoever wants to be my disciple must deny themselves and take up their cross and follow me. For whoever wants to save their life will lose it, but whoever loses their life for me and for the gospel will save it" (Mark 8:34-35).

When we fully surrender our lives, trusting God's goodness regardless of circumstances, we experience the paradoxical freedom of the redeemed. As Jim Elliot famously wrote, "He is no fool who gives what he cannot keep to gain what he cannot lose." Our souls find their anchor, our minds are renewed, and true peace becomes possible.

## Walking in Obedience

Salvation is received by grace through faith, not through our efforts or rule-following (Ephesians 2:8-9). However, those redeemed by Christ inevitably learn that obedience is the fruit of authentic faith. As James 2:17 states, "faith by itself, if it is not accompanied by action, is dead." Obedience flows from the transformation occurring in our redeemed lives.

Jesus Himself emphasized that obedience is the defining mark of a true disciple: "If you love me, you will keep my commandments" (John 14:15). Obeying God's wise precepts is the pathway to promised blessings and provision (Deuteronomy 28). But obedience is so much more than begrudgingly following rules. It is the sincere desire of the redeemed heart to please the Father who loves us perfectly.

When we grapple with the struggle of obedience, it can be instructive to revisit biblical accounts of God's people straying from His ways. Time and again, those who chased after temporal pleasures or depended on self-effort found only futility, pain, and separation from God's peace. This includes:

- The Israelites disobeying repeatedly in the wilderness after God miraculously delivered them, resulting in only Caleb and Joshua entering the Promised Land (Numbers 13-14).
- King David's lapse into adultery and murder reaping immense family strife, though his repentant heart found restoration through obedient turning back to God (2 Samuel 11-12, Psalm 51).
- Jonah's stubborn resistance leading to his being swallowed by a great fish, a crisis that eventually birthed obedient surrender to preach in Nineveh (Jonah 1-4).

In contrast, those who trusted and obeyed the Lord experienced His supernatural shalom: the peace, wholeness and favor that redemption affords. Examples include:

- Abraham's bold obedience to nearly sacrifice Isaac, affirming God's covenant blessings because "you have obeyed My voice" (Genesis 22).
- Naaman the Syrian being healed after humbly washing in the Jordan River, abandoning his prideful notions (2 Kings 5).
- Mary's simple meekness in receiving the Messiah, declaring "I am the Lord's servant...may it be done to me according to your word" (Luke 1:38).

The path to peace and the fullness of God's redemption winds through the joys and pains of learning obedience. When we surrender control and trust the Lord obediently, even when His ways defy human wisdom, we experience the blessing of drawing nearer to Him.

## Redemption's Dawn: God's Unfolding Plan

Though catastrophic, the Fall was never the end of God's redemptive story for humanity. From the first moment, He enacted His plan to reconcile sinful people back to the intended harmony and peace found only in Him.

In Genesis 3:15, God provides the first glimmer of the redemption to come, known as the Protoevangelium or "First Gospel." God tells the serpent: "I will put hostility between you and the woman, and between your seed and her seed. He will strike your head, and you will strike his heel." This cryptic promise reveals God's intention to send a Redeemer who would defeat evil and sin on our behalf.

This scarlet thread of redemption weaves throughout the Old Testament, from God clothing Adam and Eve to Abraham's willingness to sacrifice Isaac foreshadowing God's own provision of "the lamb." Each element (the garments covering shame, the ram caught in the thicket, the Passover lamb's blood, the atoning sacrifices) pointed toward the ultimate sacrifice God would make to restore peace.

Despite humanity's failures, God remained faithful to His redemptive promise. The prophets proclaimed a coming Messiah, a "Prince of Peace" who would bear our iniquities (Isaiah 9:6, 53:5). All Scripture built toward Jesus Christ's arrival as that long-awaited Redeemer.

When the angel announced Christ's birth, he proclaimed "Peace on earth!" For the God of all creation had taken human form to break sin's curse and open the path for redemption. By His perfect life, sacrificial death, and resurrection victory, Jesus defeated the powers of sin and death that held humanity captive.

As Paul declared, "For he is our peace...his purpose was to create in himself one new humanity out of the two, thus making peace" (Ephesians 2:14-15). Through Christ, humanity can be restored to God, redeemed and granted the lasting peace we inevitably long for.

## The Path of Redemption Today

While sin's curse still marks this fallen world, Christ has inaugurated the era of redemption and inner peace for all who put their faith in Him. No matter how stained by sin, defeated by circumstances, or lost in striving, redemption is available by God's grace through Jesus. He is the source of the wholeness and rest we were created for.

For any feeling trapped by sin's cycle, enslaved to ungodly patterns, or simply restless inside, the pathway is clear: Acknowledge your need, surrender to the Redeemer's Lordship, and walk in faithful obedience. From the garden until today, this is the path to experiencing the redeemed existence and perfect peace only God provides.

As you embark on this redemptive journey, anchor yourself in this truth: No amount of sin, failure or circumstance can separate you from the God who has redefined your eternity through Christ's finished work on the cross. You were created for so much more than striving apart from Him. Come, and discover the profound peace of the redeemed life.
