**A Bible Study on the Pattern of Completion**

## The Premise

Scripture counts. Not decoratively, but structurally. When a narrative takes six steps and then a seventh, the seventh is almost never simply the next item in a list. It is the turn. It is where God acts, where the thing is finished, where what was latent becomes settled.

Six is the number of man: created on the sixth day, given six days to labor, six years to sow. It is one short. It is capable, industrious, and incomplete.

Seven is the number of God: the day He blessed and sanctified, the number of the sabbath year, of the trumpets at Jericho, of the seals and bowls and trumpets in Revelation. The Hebrew *sheva* shares a root with *shava*, to swear or take an oath, and with *sova*, fullness. Seven is the number of a thing sworn, completed, brought to fullness.

This study traces one pattern through nine passages: **six by man, seven by God.**

## Part One: Pharaoh's Heart

### The Six

Pharaoh hardens his own heart six times before the text ever names God as the one doing it.

This is the hinge of the entire plague narrative, and most readers miss it because they arrive at Exodus already knowing the phrase "God hardened Pharaoh's heart" and read it backward into chapters that do not say it.

| # | Reference | Plague | What the text says |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ex 7:13 | Staff to serpent | His heart grew hard (*chazaq*); he would not listen |
| 2 | Ex 7:22 | Nile to blood | Magicians duplicated it; his heart grew hard; he did not take it to heart (v. 23) |
| 3 | Ex 8:15 | Frogs | "When Pharaoh saw that there was relief, he hardened his heart" (*hakbed*) |
| 4 | Ex 8:19 | Gnats | His own magicians said "This is the finger of God"; his heart grew hard anyway |
| 5 | Ex 8:32 | Flies | "But Pharaoh hardened his heart at this time also" |
| 6 | Ex 9:7 | Livestock | He verified Israel's cattle were spared; his heart was still hard (*kaved*) |

### The Seventh

> "And the Lord hardened the heart of Pharaoh, and he did not listen to them, just as the Lord had spoken to Moses."

Exodus 9:12, the plague of boils

First time in the narrative that God is the subject of the verb.

### Two Notes for Teaching

**On Exodus 7:14.** Some counts treat this as a seventh self-hardening, which breaks the pattern. Read it instead as the narrator's summary comment on 7:13, not a new event.

**On Exodus 4:21 and 7:3.** These are God's advance announcement of what He will do, not the act itself. The announcement precedes. The execution waits until Pharaoh has set his own course six times over.

### The Three Verbs

- *chazaq*: to strengthen, make firm, hold fast
- *kaved*: to make heavy, weighty, unresponsive
- *qashah*: to make stiff, rigid, hard

None of these mean "to install a disposition that was not there." God strengthens and fixes in place what Pharaoh chose six times. Heat does not decide what clay will become. It sets what the potter's hands already shaped.

The most damning of the six is the third. **Pharaoh hardens after relief, not after judgment.** Given exactly what he begged for, he used the breathing room to entrench. Mercy hardened him faster than plague did.

### The Mercy in the Structure

Six full opportunities. Six unforced choices. The seventh did not overrule a man who wanted to repent. It ratified a man who had refused six times and demonstrated, on the third occasion, that kindness would not move him either.

## Part Two: The Pattern Elsewhere

### Jericho: Joshua 6:3-20

Six days, one silent circuit per day. Nothing happens. No crack in the wall, no sign of progress, six days of what must have looked to Jericho like a parade and to Israel like futility.

Seventh day: seven circuits, seven priests, seven trumpets of ram's horn, then the shout. The walls fall outward. Israel never touched them.

**Teaching point:** the six days accomplished nothing visible and were not optional. Obedience without visible result is still the thing God asked for.

### Elijah on Carmel: 1 Kings 18:43-44

"Go up now, look toward the sea." Six trips up the ridge. Six reports of nothing at all. Elijah does not adjust the prayer or reconsider the promise. "Go again seven times."

Seventh trip: a cloud the size of a man's hand. Three and a half years of drought break on the seventh look.

**Teaching point:** the answer was no smaller on the seventh report than it would have been on the first. The delay formed the servant, not the cloud.

### Naaman: 2 Kings 5:10-14

Seven dips in the Jordan. Six left him a leper and, worse to his mind, a humiliated general standing in a muddy river on a prophet's say-so. The healing came only at the completed count.

**Teaching point:** this is exactly why the instruction offended him. A commander can accept a cure. He cannot easily accept a cure that requires seven acts of public foolishness. The count *was* the cure, because the count broke the man.

### The Shunammite's Son: 2 Kings 4:35

The boy sneezed seven times, and at the seventh opened his eyes. Life returns on the completing number.

### Creation: Genesis 1:31 to 2:3

Six days of forming and filling. On the seventh, God does not add to the work. He rests, blesses, and sanctifies.

**Teaching point:** the first thing God ever called holy was not a place, an object, or a person. It was time. And the seventh day was not more work completed. It was the completion itself, of a different kind than the six.

### The Sabbath Year and the Jubilee: Exodus 21:2, Leviticus 25:3-4, 25:8-10

Six years of sowing, of pruning, of service. The seventh brings release: the Hebrew slave goes free, the land lies still, the debt is cancelled.

Then seven of those cycles, forty-nine years, and the fiftieth is Jubilee. Property returns. Every man goes back to his family and his possession.

**Teaching point:** the pattern is not only a literary device. God legislated it into the economy. Israel's calendar itself preached that man's labor gets six and God's release gets the seventh.

### Revelation: Three Times Over

John builds the same architecture three separate times, and each seventh is the decisive turn rather than the last item in a sequence.

- **Seals (Rev 6:1-8:1).** Six seals, then an interlude in chapter 7, then the seventh: silence in heaven for about half an hour.
- **Trumpets (Rev 8:6-11:19).** Six trumpets, then an interlude, then the seventh: "The kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of His Christ."
- **Bowls (Rev 16).** Six bowls, then the seventh, and a voice from the throne: "It is done."

**Teaching point:** notice the interludes. Between the sixth and the seventh, in two of the three cycles, the narrative pauses. The gap before the seventh is where mercy is displayed and the sealed are numbered. Compare Pharaoh: the space between the sixth and the seventh is the last room to repent.

## Part Three: The Inverse Case

### Joash and the Arrows: 2 Kings 13:18-19

Elisha tells the king to take the arrows and strike the ground. Joash strikes three times and stops.

The prophet is angry: "You should have struck five or six times; then you would have struck Syria until you had destroyed it. But now you will strike Syria only three times."

Here the man quits before the count is full, and the incomplete number becomes the ceiling of the victory.

**Teaching point:** Joash was not disobedient. He did exactly what he was told, three times, and then decided on his own that three was enough. The sixth circuit at Jericho and the sixth trip up Carmel look identical to wasted effort right up until the seventh. Joash is what it costs to stop at the point where the effort still looks wasted.

## Part Four: Reading the Pattern Rightly

Three cautions, because this is a theme that invites abuse.

1. **It is not a formula.** Nothing in Scripture suggests that the seventh attempt at anything triggers divine action. Naaman was told seven. Joash was not given a number at all, which is precisely the point of his failure. The pattern describes how God has acted; it does not obligate Him to act on a count we supply.
2. **The six are not filler.** In every case the six are real obedience, real choice, or real labor. Pharaoh's six hardenings were genuinely his. Israel's six circuits were genuine obedience. The six days of creation are not a warm-up. God does not discard the six; He completes them.
3. **The seventh belongs to God.** This is the load-bearing claim. The walls fall without a hand on them. The cloud rises without a prayer on the seventh trip that differed from the first six. The kingdom becomes Christ's, and no one voted. What man cannot finish, God finishes, and the finishing is His alone.

## Discussion Questions

1. Exodus 8:15 says Pharaoh hardened his heart when he saw there was *relief*. Where in your own life has answered prayer made you less dependent rather than more grateful?
2. Israel walked six days around Jericho with no visible result. What are you currently doing in obedience that has produced nothing you can point to? What would it take for you to stop on day six?
3. Elijah sent his servant up seven times without changing the prayer. What does it mean to persist without renegotiating with God?
4. Naaman was humiliated by the count itself. Where has God's method, rather than God's answer, been the offense to you?
5. The sabbath year forced Israel to trust God for a full year's provision with no sowing. What is the modern equivalent of a fallow year, and why do we not take it?
6. Joash stopped at three. What is the difference between contentment and quitting early, and how would you know which one you are doing?
7. Revelation places an interlude between the sixth and the seventh. If the space before the seventh is God's last room for mercy, what should that produce in how we speak to people who have hardened themselves six times?

## Closing Thought

The pattern preaches one thing in two directions.

To the one laboring in obedience with nothing to show for it: the six are not wasted, and the seventh is not yours to manufacture. Keep walking.

To the one hardening: the six are yours. Every one of them. And there is an interlude before the seventh, which is another word for mercy, and it does not last forever.
