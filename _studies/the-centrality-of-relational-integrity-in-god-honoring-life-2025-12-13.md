Jesus does not leave the teaching on inner attitude only. He immediately ties the seriousness of **anger and contempt** to two core areas of a believer's life, demonstrating that broken relationships are **not a side issue** but are **central** to honoring God:

- **How believers worship** (vv. 23-24).
- **How believers handle conflict in everyday life** (vv. 25-26).

## Worship and Broken Relationships (vv. 23-24)

Jesus pictures someone in the very act of worship, bringing a gift to the altar, suddenly remembering that a brother or sister has something against them. The instruction is striking: **leave the gift there, go and be reconciled first, then come back and offer the gift**, showing that God values restored relationships even above outward acts of devotion.

This means:

- **Reconciliation is not optional;** it is part of genuine worship.
- God is not pleased with "religious activity" that ignores unresolved sin, harsh words, or contempt toward others.

## Urgency of Making Things Right (vv. 25-26)

Jesus then shifts to a legal picture: two people on the way to court, one urged to settle the matter quickly before it reaches the judge. If the matter is ignored, the outcome becomes severe, handed over to the judge, then the officer, then prison, with no release "till you have paid the last penny," painting a picture of **consequences that grow heavier the longer conflict is left unresolved.**

This teaches:

- **Delayed reconciliation** allows hardness, bitterness, and consequences to grow.
- Handling conflict **early and humbly** is an act of wisdom, protecting both the relationship and the heart.

## Connecting vv. 22-26: From Heart to Action

These verses show a clear flow: angry contempt in the heart (v. 22) must lead to **practical steps of reconciliation** (vv. 23-26). The one who takes Jesus seriously will not only avoid insulting others but will also **actively pursue peace,** even at personal cost or inconvenience.

In a Bible study, this can be emphasized by:

- Examining whether there is anyone "who has something against you" that you have been avoiding.
- Encouraging specific, Spirit-led steps: confession, apology, asking forgiveness, and seeking restoration **rather than waiting for the other person to move first.**

## Living This Out in a Calvary Church Context

For a Calvary-style, verse-by-verse application, the passage calls believers to:

- Let the Spirit search for hidden contempt behind "small" words like "You fool," then repent of it.
- Treat **relational integrity as part of worship**, making phone calls, having hard conversations, and seeking peace as an expression of obedience to Christ.

In this way, Matthew 5:22-26 becomes not just a warning about anger and "hell fire," but a **gracious invitation into a lifestyle of reconciled relationships** that reflects the heart.
