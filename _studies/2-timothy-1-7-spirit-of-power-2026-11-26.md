## Overview

2 Timothy 1:7 is one of the most loved verses on fear: "For God has not given us a spirit of fear, but of power and of love and of a sound mind." It is often read on its own as a word about anxiety, and the earlier study [Sound Mind]({{ '/studies/sound-mind-2026-01-23/' | relative_url }}) explores that side of it. This study reads the verse in its setting. Paul wrote it from a Roman prison to his young friend Timothy, who seems to have been naturally timid, at a time when standing with Paul and the gospel was dangerous. The verse sits between two commands: "stir up the gift of God" (1:6) and "do not be ashamed of the testimony of our Lord" (1:8). Read in context, it is God's answer to the fear that keeps believers quiet about Jesus.

## Theological Analysis

**The fear Paul names.** The word Paul uses for "fear" in verse 7 is not the reverent fear of the Lord. It is the word for timidity or cowardice, the shrinking back that makes a person hide when they should stand. Paul's other letters hint that Timothy struggled here. Paul asked the Corinthians to make sure Timothy could be with them "without fear" (1 Corinthians 16:10), and told Timothy, "Let no one despise your youth" (1 Timothy 4:12). Paul does not shame Timothy for this. He reminds him where that timidity does not come from: "God has not given us a spirit of fear."

**Stir up the gift (v. 6).** Before naming the fear, Paul tells Timothy to "stir up the gift of God which is in you." The picture is of fanning coals back into a flame. Timothy had received a gift from God for ministry, confirmed when Paul laid hands on him. Gifts can burn low through neglect, discouragement or fear. Courage is not only something we wait to feel. It is something we stir up by using what God has already given.

**What God has given (v. 7).** Against timidity Paul sets three gifts. Power is the Holy Spirit's strength, the same power Jesus promised when He said, "you shall receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you shall be witnesses to Me" (Acts 1:8). Love keeps power from becoming harsh and gives bold speech the right motive. A sound mind is self-control and clear thinking, the steadiness to speak wisely and not be ruled by panic. Each one answers a different side of fear. Fear says we are too weak; God gives power. Fear makes us protect ourselves; God gives love for others. Fear clouds our judgment; God gives a sound mind.

**Do not be ashamed (v. 8).** The word "therefore" ties verse 8 to verse 7. Because God has given this Spirit, Timothy must not be ashamed "of the testimony of our Lord, nor of me His prisoner." In Rome, the gospel of a crucified Jew sounded foolish, and Paul's chains made association with him risky. Many in Asia had already turned away from Paul (1:15). Yet Paul invites Timothy to "share with me in the sufferings for the gospel according to the power of God." Courage for witness does not come from avoiding suffering. It comes from the power of God within it.

**Paul's own example (v. 12).** Paul does not ask Timothy for anything he is unwilling to do himself. "I am not ashamed, for I know whom I have believed." His confidence rests on a Person. He knows Jesus, and he is persuaded that Jesus will keep what Paul has entrusted to Him. That is the root of boldness: not confidence in ourselves, but confidence in the One we know.

## Historical & Cultural Context

2 Timothy is most likely Paul's last letter, written from his final imprisonment in Rome in the mid 60s AD, during the reign of Nero. After the great fire of Rome in AD 64, Nero blamed Christians, and many were arrested and put to death. Unlike Paul's earlier house arrest (Acts 28:30-31), this imprisonment was harsh. Paul expected to die soon (2 Timothy 4:6-8). Visiting him or being known as his friend carried real risk.

Timothy was then leading the church in Ephesus. He had traveled with Paul for years and was like a son to him (1:2). His faith had first lived in his grandmother Lois and his mother Eunice (1:5). Paul's letter mixes deep affection with urgent charges, preparing Timothy to carry on the work after Paul's death. In chapter 1 Paul contrasts those in Asia who turned away from him with Onesiphorus, who "was not ashamed of my chain" and searched Rome until he found him (1:16-17).

## Practical Application

1. Name the gift God has given you, whether teaching, encouragement, service or simply your testimony, and look for one way to use it this week.
2. When you feel timid about speaking of Jesus, remember that timidity is not from God. Ask Him for power, love and a sound mind before you speak.
3. Let love shape your boldness. Ask how your words can serve the person in front of you, not only how to say the truth.
4. Think of someone who is suffering or isolated for their faith and stand with them by a visit, a call or a note, as Onesiphorus did for Paul.
5. Pray Acts 1:8 for yourself: that the Holy Spirit would make you a witness where you live and work.
6. Write out 2 Timothy 1:12 and keep it where you will see it: "I know whom I have believed."

## Greek & Hebrew Word Study

1. **"anazōpyreō" [Greek G329]: "stir up"**
   To rekindle or fan into flame. It pictures glowing coals being blown back into a fire. Gifts and courage can grow cold and must be stirred.

2. **"charisma" [Greek G5486]: "gift"**
   A gift of grace freely given by God. Timothy's gift for ministry was God's own provision for the work God had called him to.

3. **"deilia" [Greek G1167]: "fear"**
   Timidity, cowardice, shrinking fear. This is the only place the noun appears in the New Testament. It is not the reverent fear of God but the fear that makes us hold back.

4. **"dynamis" [Greek G1411]: "power"**
   Strength, ability, mighty power. It is the same word Jesus used for the power of the Spirit that would make His disciples witnesses (Acts 1:8).

5. **"sōphronismos" [Greek G4995]: "sound mind"**
   Self-control, sound judgment, a disciplined mind. See the study Sound Mind for a fuller treatment of this word.

6. **"epaischynomai" [Greek G1870]: "be ashamed"**
   To feel shame about something or someone, to be embarrassed to be associated with it. Paul uses it in Romans 1:16 and three times in this chapter (1:8, 1:12, 1:16).

7. **"synkakopatheō" [Greek G4777]: "share in sufferings"**
   To suffer hardship together with someone. Paul invites Timothy into fellowship with him in the costs of the gospel.

## Apologetics & Deeper Study

2 Timothy offers a moving historical window into the early church. Its author writes from prison, expecting execution, and urges his friend not to be ashamed of a crucified Lord. A man facing death under Nero had every reason to recant if the gospel were a story he had invented. Instead Paul writes, "I know whom I have believed." The steadiness of the apostles under persecution is part of the historical case for the truth of their message.

The letter also shows the Holy Spirit's role in Christian courage. The New Testament does not ask believers to be naturally fearless. It promises the Spirit, who gives power for witness (Acts 1:8) and who replaces the spirit of bondage to fear with the Spirit of adoption (Romans 8:15). Christian courage is not mere personality. It is the life of God's Spirit at work in ordinary, sometimes timid people.

### Common Objections & Responses

1. **Objection:** "2 Timothy 1:7 means a Christian should never feel fear."

   **Response:** Paul is not saying believers never feel afraid. He is saying that timidity is not from God and does not have to rule us. God gives power, love and a sound mind so we can act faithfully even when we feel fear.

2. **Objection:** "Boldness is for people with outgoing personalities, not for someone like me."

   **Response:** Timothy appears to have been naturally timid, and Paul still called him to bold ministry. The power Paul describes comes from God's Spirit, not from personality. Quiet people can be bold witnesses.

3. **Objection:** "Being associated with Christianity today can cost me socially or professionally. It is wiser to keep quiet."

   **Response:** Paul acknowledged the cost and invited Timothy to share in it "according to the power of God." Wisdom shapes how we speak, but Scripture never treats silence about Jesus as wisdom. Love for others and confidence in Christ call us to speak.

### Supporting Evidence

1. **Timothy's Background in Paul's Letters**
   1 Corinthians 16:10 and 1 Timothy 4:12 suggest Timothy's youth and possible timidity, giving 2 Timothy 1:7 a personal and pastoral setting.

2. **The Historical Setting Under Nero**
   Roman historians, including Tacitus, record Nero's persecution of Christians after the fire of AD 64, matching the dangerous setting reflected in 2 Timothy.

3. **The Promise of the Spirit's Power**
   Acts 1:8 and the courage of the apostles throughout Acts show the same pattern Paul describes: the Holy Spirit giving ordinary believers power to witness.
