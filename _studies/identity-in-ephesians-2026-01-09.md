In the book of Ephesians, the Apostle Paul provides a systematic breakdown of the believer's identity. These identities are primarily "positional": describing who a person is because of their union with Christ, rather than their own performance.

In the ESV, these identities can be categorized into three main levels: Individual, Corporate (The Church), and Practical (The Walk).

## 1. Individual Identities (Chapter 1)

The first chapter of Ephesians is often called the "Identity Charter" of the New Testament. It lists the spiritual blessings that define a believer's status.

- **Blessed (1:3):** Possessor of "every spiritual blessing in the heavenly places."
- **Chosen (1:4):** Selected before the foundation of the world.
- **Holy and Blameless (1:4):** Set apart and without accusation in God's sight.
- **Adopted (1:5):** Legally brought into God's family as sons/daughters.
- **Redeemed and Forgiven (1:7):** Bought back from slavery to sin and cleared of guilt.
- **Heirs (1:11):** Recipients of a divine inheritance.
- **Sealed (1:13):** Permanently marked by the Holy Spirit as God's possession.

## 2. Corporate Identities (Chapters 2-3)

Identity in Ephesians is not just individual; it is deeply communal. Paul describes how Christ's work redefines how we relate to others and the spiritual realm.

- **One New Man (2:15):** Part of a new humanity that reconciles former enemies (specifically Jew and Gentile).
- **Fellow Citizens (2:19):** Legal residents of God's kingdom, no longer "strangers and aliens."
- **Members of the Household (2:19):** Part of God's immediate family.
- **Living Temple (2:21):** Being built together into a dwelling place for God's Spirit.
- **The Body of Christ (1:23; 4:12):** A living organism where Christ is the Head and believers are functional members.
- **The Bride of Christ (5:25-27):** The object of Christ's sacrificial love and future glory.

## 3. Practical & Functional Identities (Chapters 4-6)

In the second half of the letter, Paul transitions from who we are to how we live out that identity.

- **Saints (1:1; 4:12):** "Set apart ones" called to live in holiness.
- **God's Workmanship (2:10):** A "masterpiece" (Greek: poiēma) created for good works.
- **Ambassadors (6:20):** Representatives of the King, even in "chains" or difficulty.
- **Children of Light (5:8):** People who expose darkness and reflect God's truth.
- **Spiritual Warrior (6:10-17):** One who is "strong in the Lord" and equipped with divine armor.

## Statistical Correlation: "In Christ"

The foundation of every identity listed above is the phrase "in Christ" (or "in Him").

- **Occurrence:** Versions of this phrase appear roughly 36 times in Ephesians, the highest density in any New Testament book.
- **Theological Meaning:** Paul argues that you cannot have the identity of a child, saint, or heir without the position of being united to Christ.
