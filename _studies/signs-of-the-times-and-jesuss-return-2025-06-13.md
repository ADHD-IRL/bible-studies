**Overall Theme:** Understanding God's prophetic word concerning the end times and Jesus's return, and how to live faithfully and discerningly in light of current global events.

## Study Goals

- To gain a deeper understanding of biblical prophecies related to the end times.
- To compare ancient Jewish messianic expectations with Jesus's actual ministry and prophecies.
- To thoughtfully consider how current events (natural disasters, violence, moral decline) might align with biblical signs.
- To learn how to approach end-times discussions with discernment, avoiding sensationalism.
- To encourage practical responses of faith, holiness, and evangelism.

## Session 1: Laying the Foundation, Prophecy and Expectations

**Goal:** To understand the nature of biblical prophecy, why it's important, and the historical context of Jewish messianic expectations.

**Key Scripture Passages:**

- **Deuteronomy 18:21-22:** Discerning true vs. false prophets.
- **2 Peter 1:19-21:** The divine origin of prophecy.
- **Isaiah 9:6-7:** A classic Messianic prophecy about a king and peace.
- **Isaiah 53:** The Suffering Servant prophecy.
- **Daniel 2, 7:** Prophecies concerning future kingdoms and the Messiah's kingdom.

**Discussion Questions:**

1. What does the Bible teach us about the purpose of prophecy? Why do you think God reveals future events?

   **Leader's Guide Answer:** The Bible teaches that prophecy is God's way of revealing His sovereign plan, demonstrating His power over history, and confirming His Word. It's not primarily for satisfying curiosity about the future, but to affirm His character, call people to repentance, give hope, warn of judgment, and prepare believers for what is to come. God reveals future events to strengthen our faith, motivate righteous living, and provide a roadmap for His ultimate redemption.

2. In the first century, what were the primary expectations of the Messiah among the Jewish people, based on their understanding of the Old Testament? (e.g., political liberator, king, rebuilder of the Temple, usher of global peace).

   **Leader's Guide Answer:** In the first century, many Jews expected the Messiah to be a political and military leader (a descendant of King David) who would liberate Israel from Roman oppression, restore their national sovereignty, rebuild the Temple, gather all dispersed Jews, and usher in a golden age of peace and justice on Earth where all nations would acknowledge the God of Israel. They anticipated a powerful, conquering king.

3. How did Jesus's ministry and eventual death on the cross not align with some of those prevalent Jewish expectations at the time?

   **Leader's Guide Answer:** Jesus's ministry did not align with the predominant political expectations. He spoke of a spiritual kingdom, emphasized repentance and inner transformation rather than military conquest, and His ultimate act was a humiliating death on a cross, not a triumphant overthrow of Roman rule. He focused on individual salvation from sin rather than immediate national liberation. This was a "stumbling block" for many who expected a different kind of Messiah.

4. Why might it have been difficult for many Jews to accept Jesus as the Messiah, given their strong beliefs and interpretations of prophecy?

   **Leader's Guide Answer:** It was difficult because Jesus did not fulfill their immediate, tangible expectations of a conquering king. His suffering and death contradicted the image of a victorious political leader. Additionally, the concept of a divine Messiah, or God becoming man, clashed with their strict monotheistic understanding of God's absolute singularity. Their interpretation of certain prophecies focused solely on the victorious aspects, often overlooking or not applying the "suffering servant" prophecies (like Isaiah 53) to the Messiah himself.

5. What lessons can we learn from this historical context about approaching prophecy today? (e.g., avoiding preconceived notions, understanding different interpretations).

   **Leader's Guide Answer:** We learn the importance of approaching prophecy with humility and an open mind, avoiding rigid preconceived notions or narrow interpretations. We must be careful not to impose our own cultural or political expectations onto God's plan. It highlights the need to study all relevant biblical passages comprehensively, recognizing that God's ways and timings are higher than ours. It also shows that the fulfillment of prophecy can occur in ways we don't initially anticipate.

**Activity/Application:**

- Have participants briefly share one thing they hope to gain from this study.
- Discuss the importance of humility and careful study when approaching prophetic texts.

**Preparation for Next Session:** Read Matthew 24, Mark 13, and Luke 21. Pay attention to what Jesus says about the "signs of the end."

## Session 2: Jesus's Own Words, the Olivet Discourse

**Goal:** To examine Jesus's direct prophecies about the end times and His return, focusing on the "birth pains" analogy.

**Key Scripture Passages:**

- Matthew 24:1-14
- Mark 13:1-13
- Luke 21:5-19

**Discussion Questions:**

1. What questions did the disciples ask Jesus that prompted this discourse on the end times? (Matthew 24:3)

   **Leader's Guide Answer:** The disciples asked Jesus three specific questions: "Tell us, when will these things be? What will be the sign of Your coming? And what will be the sign of the end of the age?" They were interested in the timing of the destruction of the Temple, the sign of His return, and the end of the current age.

2. Jesus mentions "wars and rumors of wars," "nation rising against nation," "famines," "earthquakes," and "pestilences." What is significant about how He describes these as "the beginning of birth pains"? What does this analogy suggest about their nature and progression?

   **Leader's Guide Answer:** Describing these as "the beginning of birth pains" (or "birth pangs") is significant because it suggests several things:

   - **Increasing Frequency:** Like contractions, they will occur more often.
   - **Increasing Intensity:** They will become more severe and widespread.
   - **Sign of Imminence:** They indicate that the ultimate event (the "birth" of a new age, Jesus's return) is drawing nearer, but it's not the end itself.
   - **Natural Process:** While difficult, birth pains lead to something new and glorious. It's a process, not a sudden, singular event from the very beginning.

3. Jesus also warns about false Christs and false prophets. How do these warnings apply to our modern context?

   **Leader's Guide Answer:** These warnings are highly relevant today. In our modern context, "false Christs" can refer to individuals claiming divine authority or messianic roles, while "false prophets" encompass those who distort biblical truth, preach doctrines that deviate from the core Gospel, or offer deceptive spiritual guidance. This could include cult leaders, prosperity gospel preachers, or anyone who subtly undermines the supremacy and truth of Jesus Christ through their teachings. The ease of information spread through technology makes these warnings even more pertinent, as false teachings can spread rapidly.

4. What does Jesus say about the persecution of believers in the end times? How might we see this playing out globally today?

   **Leader's Guide Answer:** Jesus explicitly states that His followers will be persecuted, hated, arrested, and even put to death for His name's sake (Matthew 24:9-10, Mark 13:9-13, Luke 21:12-17). Globally today, we see this playing out in various forms: Christians facing severe persecution, imprisonment, violence, and martyrdom in many parts of the world (e.g., in countries with authoritarian regimes or extremist groups). Even in more developed nations, there can be increasing social pressure, discrimination, or marginalization for holding biblical convictions.

5. In Matthew 24:14, Jesus states: "And this gospel of the kingdom will be preached in the whole world as a testimony to all nations, and then the end will come." How does this prophecy relate to current global missionary efforts and technological advancements?

   **Leader's Guide Answer:** This prophecy is seen by many as directly correlating with modern global missionary efforts. With advancements in technology (internet, satellite TV, social media, translation tools), the Gospel message can now reach parts of the world that were previously inaccessible. Many Christian organizations are actively working to translate the Bible into every language and send missionaries to every unreached people group, seeing this as a direct command that needs to be fulfilled before Christ's return. It suggests a significant global evangelistic thrust will precede the very end.

**Activity/Application:**

- Discuss the difference between "signs" that point to an approaching event and "predicting" the exact timing.
- Encourage participants to identify one practical step they can take to "be ready" as Jesus instructs (e.g., consistent prayer, studying His word, sharing their faith).

**Preparation for Next Session:** Reflect on current global events (news, climate reports, social trends) and consider how they might connect to these biblical themes.

## Session 3: Connecting Prophecy to Current Events, With Discernment

**Goal:** To responsibly connect biblical end-times themes to current global events, emphasizing careful interpretation and avoiding sensationalism.

**Key Scripture Passages:**

- **2 Timothy 3:1-5:** The moral state of humanity in the last days.
- **Romans 8:22-23:** Creation groaning.
- **Revelation (select passages, e.g., 6:1-8 on the four horsemen; 13 on the beast/Antichrist system):** Broader apocalyptic visions.
- **Ezekiel 36:24; 37:21-22:** Prophecies concerning the restoration of Israel.

**Discussion Questions:**

1. Based on 2 Timothy 3:1-5, how do you see the moral and spiritual climate of the world (or even your own culture) aligning with these descriptions of the "last days"?

   **Leader's Guide Answer:** This passage describes people as "lovers of themselves, lovers of money, boastful, proud, abusive, disobedient to their parents, ungrateful, unholy, without love, unforgiving, slanderous, without self-control, brutal, not lovers of the good, treacherous, rash, conceited, lovers of pleasure rather than lovers of God, having a form of godliness but denying its power." Many observe that these traits are increasingly prevalent in modern society, evident in individualism, consumerism, social media culture, breakdown of family structures, and a rise in narcissism and moral relativism, even within some segments of religious communities.

2. The Bible speaks of creation groaning and being subjected to futility (Romans 8:22-23). How might changes in Earth's weather patterns and increases in natural disasters (like wildfires, floods, extreme heat/cold) be interpreted in light of this concept?

   **Leader's Guide Answer:** This verse suggests that the entire creation suffers and longs for redemption due to the effects of sin (the "fall" in Genesis). Changes in weather patterns and increased natural disasters can be interpreted as manifestations of this "groaning." While natural phenomena have always existed, many believe their increasing frequency and intensity today indicate a heightened state of creation's suffering, aligning with the "birth pains" Jesus described. It's a reminder of the brokenness of the world and the anticipation of a renewed creation.

3. Many Christians see the re-establishment of the state of Israel in 1948 as a significant prophetic fulfillment. Why is this event considered important in some end-times views?

   **Leader's Guide Answer:** For many, especially those holding a dispensationalist view of prophecy, the re-establishment of Israel as a nation in 1948, after nearly 2,000 years of dispersion, is a direct fulfillment of biblical prophecies (e.g., Ezekiel 36-37, Deuteronomy 30). It's seen as a major "super sign" that sets the stage for further end-times events, signaling that God is actively working in history according to His prophetic timetable concerning His chosen people. It implies that the "times of the Gentiles" are drawing to a close.

4. How can we, as a group, discuss current events in light of prophecy without falling into sensationalism, fear-mongering, or setting specific dates (which the Bible warns against)?

   **Leader's Guide Answer:** Key strategies include:

   - **Focus on Discipleship:** Emphasize that the signs are a call to spiritual readiness and evangelism, not fear or panic.
   - **Avoid Date Setting:** Remind the group that Jesus explicitly said no one knows the day or hour (Mark 13:32). Failed predictions erode credibility.
   - **Broad Trends vs. Specific Events:** Discuss general trends (wars, famines, moral decline) as signs, rather than pinpointing every specific disaster or political event as a direct, singular fulfillment.
   - **Biblical Interpretation First:** Ground discussions firmly in scripture, rather than relying on news headlines or popular theories.
   - **Humility and Love:** Acknowledge that interpretations vary and maintain a spirit of unity and love, even when there are disagreements on specific prophetic details.

5. What are some of the potential dangers of becoming overly focused on speculative end-times predictions?

   **Leader's Guide Answer:** Potential dangers include:

   - **Disillusionment and Loss of Faith:** When predictions fail, it can lead to disappointment, cynicism, and even apostasy.
   - **Neglect of Present Responsibilities:** An unhealthy focus on "the end" can lead to neglecting family, work, community, or social justice issues, assuming they no longer matter.
   - **Fear and Anxiety:** Speculation can breed unhealthy fear rather than the peace God offers.
   - **Divisiveness:** Rigid adherence to specific timelines or interpretations can cause unnecessary division within the church.
   - **Legalism or Presumption:** It can lead to a legalistic mindset or a presumptuous attitude about knowing God's precise plan.

**Activity/Application:**

- Have a brief, non-alarmist discussion about recent news headlines and consider which ones might resonate with the "signs" we've studied.
- Pray for those affected by natural disasters and violence, and for wisdom to live faithfully in these times.

**Preparation for Next Session:** Read 1 Thessalonians 5:1-11 and Titus 2:11-13.

## Session 4: Living in Light of Prophecy, Our Response

**Goal:** To understand the practical implications of end-times prophecy for Christian living, fostering hope, holiness, and active engagement rather than fear or passivity.

**Key Scripture Passages:**

- **1 Thessalonians 5:1-11:** Being alert and sober.
- **Titus 2:11-14:** Living godly lives while awaiting the blessed hope.
- **Matthew 25:14-30:** The Parable of the Talents (being productive).
- **2 Peter 3:8-14:** God's patience and our call to holiness.

**Discussion Questions:**

1. According to 1 Thessalonians 5, how should believers live in anticipation of the Lord's return? What does it mean to be "alert and sober" rather than "asleep"?

   **Leader's Guide Answer:** Believers should live as "children of light and children of the day." To be "alert" means to be spiritually awake, aware of the times, and discerning about spiritual realities, rather than being caught off guard by the unexpected return of Christ. To be "sober" means to be self-controlled, clear-headed, and not consumed by worldly distractions or anxieties. It implies readiness through prayer, faith, and righteous living, actively pursuing God rather than being spiritually complacent.

2. Titus 2:11-14 speaks of God's grace teaching us to "say 'No' to ungodliness and worldly passions, and to live self-controlled, upright and godly lives in this present age." How does the hope of Jesus's return motivate us to live more holy lives?

   **Leader's Guide Answer:** The hope of Jesus's return motivates us to holy living because we want to be found blameless and prepared when He appears. Knowing that He will judge righteously and that we will stand before Him inspires us to shed sinful habits and embrace righteousness. It emphasizes that our earthly lives are temporary and that our true citizenship is in heaven, prompting us to align our actions with eternal values rather than fleeting worldly pleasures. It is a powerful incentive for sanctification.

3. Jesus emphasized faithfulness and productivity (e.g., Parable of the Talents). How does this principle apply to our lives as we await His return, counteracting any temptation towards apathy or withdrawing from the world?

   **Leader's Guide Answer:** The Parable of the Talents illustrates that we are entrusted with gifts, resources, and opportunities by God, and we are expected to use them faithfully for His kingdom until He returns. This directly counters apathy or withdrawing from the world. It means actively engaging in our callings, serving others, developing our talents, and investing in eternal matters. Our waiting is not passive; it's an active, productive anticipation of our Master's return, where we desire to hear, "Well done, good and faithful servant!"

4. Why is it important to remember that "the Lord is not slow in keeping his promise... instead he is patient with you, not wanting anyone to perish, but everyone to come to repentance" (2 Peter 3:9)? How does this affect our attitude towards evangelism?

   **Leader's Guide Answer:** This verse highlights God's immense patience and His loving desire for all people to turn to Him. It's crucial because it corrects any impatience or frustration about the timing of Christ's return, reminding us that God's delay is for the sake of salvation. This understanding should profoundly impact our attitude toward evangelism, fostering urgency, compassion, and a commitment to sharing the Gospel with others, knowing that every moment is an opportunity for someone to repent and be saved before judgment comes.

5. What is the ultimate hope for believers regarding Jesus's return? How can this hope provide peace and strength amidst a world experiencing "birth pains"?

   **Leader's Guide Answer:** The ultimate hope for believers is the glorious appearing of Jesus Christ, His triumph over evil, the establishment of His righteous kingdom, the resurrection of believers, and eternal life with Him in a new heavens and new earth free from sin, suffering, and death (Titus 2:13, Revelation 21-22). This hope provides immense peace because we know that God is in control, that our suffering is temporary, and that a perfect future awaits us. It gives us strength to persevere through current challenges ("birth pains"), knowing that our redemption is drawing near and that God's promises are absolutely certain.

**Activity/Application:**

- Spend time in prayer, thanking God for His faithfulness and asking for wisdom, discernment, and courage to live out these truths.
- Encourage group members to identify one personal commitment they will make based on what they've learned throughout the study.

## Concluding Thoughts for the Group

This study has aimed to equip us with a balanced, biblical understanding of end-times prophecy. While signs may increase in our world, our focus remains on Jesus Christ: His first coming, His present reign, and His glorious return. Let's strive to be discerning, grounded in God's Word, and actively living out our faith, sharing the hope we have with a world in need.
